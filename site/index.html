What it actually feels like to use

The Ashfall Crown — NovelForge
Write

Stay inside the story.

Real .docx files, one per scene, open in Word or in-app and always in sync. Focus mode, distraction-free mode, ghost mode for perfectionists, typewriter scrolling — every one of them exists to get the interface out of your way. Live spelling and grammar are tuned to your manuscript's own names and invented words, not a generic dictionary.

Corkboard
Plan

Know where the story is going.

Nine outline frameworks — Three-Act, Save the Cat, Snowflake, Seven-Point, Story Circle, Hero's Journey, Romancing the Beat, Mystery/Crime, Freytag. Switch between them freely; each one's answers are kept, so trying a different shape costs nothing. The corkboard shows every scene as an index card you can drag to reorder, so the shape of the whole book is visible at once.

Story Graph — The Ashfall Crown
Understand

See what your manuscript knows.

NovelForge reads your whole manuscript and builds a map of it — every character, place, object and thread, and every scene each one appears in. It finds them two ways: links you set by hand, and names it finds in your actual prose, which is how it notices what you forgot to link. A 15-point continuity checker runs on top of the same graph — dropped plot threads, dates running backwards, a character who appears before they're born.

Map Maker — Gnashrok and Beyond
Build worlds

Draw the world behind the words.

Press "Surprise Me" for a complete fantasy world — coastlines, kingdoms, rivers, roads, dozens of named settlements — built from the system's own randomness, with a seed you can save and regenerate exactly. Prefer to draw it yourself? Freehand coastlines, 13 terrain types and 20 pin types are there too, in four art styles from aged parchment to a clean ink atlas.

Protect your work

Your novel stays yours.

Nothing here depends on a server staying online, a company staying in business, or a subscription staying paid. Full undo/redo covers structural edits too — delete a chapter, get it back, files included. This is the whole reason NovelForge writes real Word documents in the first place: if the tool vanished tomorrow, your novel would still be sitting there, exactly as readable as it is today.

Getting started

Three steps, no technical background needed

1

Install Python

Free, from python.org — tick "Add python.exe to PATH" during setup. One-time, a couple of minutes.

2

Download NovelForge

Grab the latest release from GitHub and unzip it anywhere on your PC — your Desktop or Documents folder is fine.

3

Double-click Write.bat

That's it. It finds Python on its own and opens straight into the app. File → New Novel... to begin.

🪟Windows 10 & 11
🍎macOS — not yet
🐧Linux — not yet

NovelForge's engine is plain Python, but its interface is built with Tkinter and has only ever been tested on Windows. macOS/Linux support is a real possibility, not a promise — open an issue if that's what would get you using it.
