Learn how to find, add, rename, reorder and delete things in the binder, the tree on the left of the main window. Most rows are real files or folders on your disk (outline beats and timeline events live in the small project.json file instead), and every delete can be undone.

What the binder shows

The top level of the tree has twelve groups, always in this order: Manuscript, Outline & Beats, Characters, Locations, Items, Factions, Plot Threads, World Bible, Maps, Timeline, Notes & Research and Publishing. A number on the right of each row tells you what is inside it:

  • Manuscript shows the word count of the whole book. Under it are your chapters, each with its word count, and under each chapter its scenes, each with theirs. A novel with three chapters or fewer opens with every chapter expanded.
  • Outline & Beats shows how many beats of your chosen story structure you have marked done, such as 3/15. Each beat has a check mark when it is done and a dot when you have written an answer for it but not finished it.
  • Characters, Locations, Items, Factions and Plot Threads show how many sheets there are. A character who is a point-of-view character wears a small diamond after the name.
  • World Bible, Publishing, Maps and Notes & Research list the Word documents and maps in the matching folder of the project. A Word document you drop into one of those folders shows up here in a muted colour with the label docx.
  • Timeline lists your timeline events, each with its story date.

The colour of a scene

Each scene has a small dot showing its status: Outline, Draft, Revised, Needs Work or Final. You set the status in the inspector on the right. A scene you have switched off for compiling is shown muted, with (excluded) after its name. A chapter switched off the same way is shown muted.

Choosing things

Click a row to select it. The middle pane and the inspector change to show it. A scene opens in the editor. Double-click does something different for each kind of row:

  • A character, location, item, faction or plot-thread sheet, a note or a document opens in Word.
  • A map opens in the map maker.
  • An outline beat opens the outline window, and a timeline event opens the timeline.

To jump to something without the mouse, click in the binder and start typing its name: the first row whose name begins with the letters you type is selected, and the buffer resets after about a second.

Adding

Everything is added from the Add menu, and each addition opens a small prompt:

  • Chapter (Ctrl+Shift+C) creates a folder inside 00 Manuscript named like 01 Chapter One, ready for its scenes.
  • Scene (Ctrl+N) goes into the chapter you have selected, or the chapter of the scene you have selected, or the last chapter if neither applies. It becomes a Word file named like 01-02 The Ferry.docx: chapter number, scene number, then the title.
  • Character, Location, Item, Faction and Plot Thread each create a Word sheet with every field worth recording. A character also asks for a role.
  • Note and Research Note each create a Word document, in the project's 10 Notes and 09 Research folders, and both are listed under Notes & Research. Timeline Event adds an entry to the timeline.

Renaming and reordering

Right-click a row for a menu of what you can do with that kind of thing. The scene menu, for example, offers Rename, Move Up, Move Down, Move to Chapter, Open in Word, Drafts, Versions, Craft Check, What depends on this? and Delete. A chapter's menu adds Add Scene and has the same rename, move and delete entries.

Renaming changes the label, not the file. The Word document keeps the file name it was created with; only the name shown in NovelForge changes. Leave the files themselves alone in Explorer: NovelForge finds each one by the path it recorded, and Tools → Verify This Project reports any file it can no longer find.

Move Up and Move Down shift a scene or chapter one place. To see the whole book and reorder in bulk, open the corkboard (Ctrl+K) and drag a card onto another, or onto a chapter heading to move it to that chapter.

Deleting, and getting it back

Choose Delete from the right-click menu. Before it asks, NovelForge checks what else in your story depends on a scene you are about to remove, and lists it in the question. You then choose one of three answers:

  • Yes removes the item and moves its Word documents to the project's _Trash folder. They are not destroyed.
  • No removes it from the binder but leaves the files exactly where they were.
  • Cancel does nothing.

Either way, Ctrl+Z puts it back, files included. Edit → Undo names the last thing it would take back, and Edit → Open the Trash Folder shows what is in _Trash. Nothing in the trash is ever removed automatically.

Inside the writing area, Ctrl+Z undoes your typing. Everywhere else it undoes the last change to the project. Ctrl+Alt+Z undoes the last project change even while your cursor is in the text.

Files that arrive from outside

Because the binder mirrors real folders, you can add to it from Explorer. Put a .docx file into a chapter folder and choose Manuscript → Import Loose .docx Files; each file becomes a scene with its word count read in. Edit a scene in Word and press F2 (File → Reload from Word) to bring the change into NovelForge. NovelForge also checks for changed files whenever it opens a novel, and only re-reads the documents whose modified time has changed.
